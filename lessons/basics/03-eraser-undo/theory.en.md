# Eraser and undo

On paper an ink blot is forever. In the editor any mistake can go away in two ways: erase it or undo it.

## The eraser

The eraser (**E**) is a brush in reverse. It has the same settings:

- **size** — how much is erased in one pass;
- **hardness** — a crisp or soft edge of the erased spot;
- **opacity** — erases completely or only weakens.

A soft eraser with low opacity is handy for "toning down" places that are too dark without erasing them completely.

On the background layer the eraser paints with the background colour; on an ordinary layer it makes pixels transparent. More on layers in the “Layers” lesson.

## The history

The editor remembers every action: a stroke, a fill, a new layer. That is the history.

| Action | Keys |
|---|---|
| Undo | **Ctrl+Z** |
| Redo | **Ctrl+Shift+Z** |
| The “History” panel | Window › History |

You can undo many steps in a row. In the “History” panel you can click any step to go back to it.

If you do something new after undoing, the undone steps disappear: the history takes a new branch.

## Erase or undo?

- The mistake just happened — **undo**: it is faster and more precise.
- The mistake was long ago, and a lot of good work came after it — **erase** only the mistake.

## Sensei's advice

Courage in drawing comes when a mistake costs nothing. Try bold strokes and unexpected colours — Ctrl+Z is always close.

# Brush: size, hardness, colour

A digital brush is a stamp the editor places many times along the movement of the mouse or pen. The stamp has dozens of settings, but a drawing rests on three.

## Size

Size is the diameter of the stamp in pixels. A large brush lays down patches and the background, a small one details and lines.

- **[** — make the brush smaller, **]** — larger.
- Start big. A small brush at the beginning leads to "polishing" details while the overall form has not been found yet.

## Hardness

Hardness is about the edge of the stamp:

- **100%** — a crisp edge, like a felt-tip pen;
- **0%** — the edge melts like ink on damp washi paper.

A soft brush is handy for smooth transitions of light and shadow — the «Light and shadow» category is all about them. A hard one is for silhouettes and crisp borders.

**Shift+[** and **Shift+]** change the hardness from the keyboard.

## Colour

There are two squares on the toolbar: the top one is the **foreground colour**, the brush paints with it; the bottom one is the **background colour**, used, for example, to fill an erased background.

- A click on a square opens the palette. A colour can be entered as a code: `#BC3823` is vermilion, `#16161A` is ink.
- **X** swaps the foreground and background colours, **D** brings back black and white.
- The eyedropper (**I**, or Alt with the brush selected) picks a colour right from the canvas.

## Opacity and pressure

The brush opacity sets how strongly one stroke lands. At 30% strokes build up in layers, like watercolour glazes. With a graphics tablet, pen pressure changes the size or opacity during the stroke.

## Sensei's advice

A stroke is born in the shoulder, not the wrist. Draw a long line with the whole arm in one movement: that is how it comes out alive and confident.

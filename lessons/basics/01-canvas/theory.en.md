# Canvas and zoom

The canvas is the sheet you draw on. It has a size in pixels, for example 1200 × 800. That size is set when the document is created and does not change when you zoom in or out.

## Zoom is a magnifier

Zoom shows how large you see the canvas:

- **100%** — one pixel of the picture takes one pixel of the screen. This is how the picture will look "in real life".
- **200%, 400%** — pixels look larger. Handy for small details: an eye, an edge, a highlight.
- **Below 100%** — you see the whole composition at once.

Experienced artists switch all the time: they work a detail up close and then zoom out at once to check the picture as a whole. From a distance it is easier to see that a form has "drifted" or that the shapes no longer hold the composition.

## Tools

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| The “Zoom” tool | key **Z** |
| Zoom in | a click with “Zoom” or **Ctrl+=** |
| Zoom out | **Alt+click** or **Ctrl+−** |
| Show the whole canvas | **Ctrl+0** |
| Actual size, 100% | **Ctrl+1** |
| The “Hand” tool | key **H** |
| The “Hand” for a moment | hold **Space** |

## The “Hand” on Space

When the canvas is zoomed in, part of it goes past the edge of the window. The “Hand” moves the view without touching the drawing. Switching to it from the menu is slow, so almost everyone draws like this: hold Space, move the canvas with the mouse, let go of Space — and the brush is back in hand.

## Sensei's advice

Zoom out more often than you want to. Five minutes up close without a look at the whole, and small successes stop adding up to a picture.

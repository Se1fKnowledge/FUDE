# Light source and form

The eye understands volume only through light. A circle drawn as an outline is flat. The same circle with a lit side and a shadow side becomes a ball.

## The first rule

Before laying in tone, answer the question: **where is the light coming from?** Everything else depends on it: where it is lighter, where it is darker, where the shadow falls. If the source "wanders" around the picture, the form falls apart even when every part is drawn well.

In this lesson the sun is at the top left. So:

- the top left part of the ball faces the light — it is **light**;
- the bottom right part faces away from the light — it is **in shadow**.

## Two sides of a form

Any lit object splits into two large parts:

1. **Light** — everything the rays reach directly.
2. **Shadow** — everything they do not reach.

The border between them is called the **terminator**. On a ball it is an arc that follows its roundness. On a cube it is an edge. On a face it is a line running along the cheekbone and the side of the nose.

## Order of work

1. Fill the form with a **mid tone** — neither light nor dark.
2. Find where the light comes from.
3. **Lighten** the side towards the light.
4. Only then — shadows, halftones and details (the next lessons).

Starting from the middle is handy: both light and dark show at once, not against white paper.

## A soft brush

The tone on a ball changes smoothly, so you need a soft brush: hardness 0%, opacity 30–50%. A few light strokes give an even transition; one strong stroke gives a blotch.

## Sensei's advice

Squint at the picture. The details disappear and the large patches of light and shadow remain. If the form reads when you squint, you are on the right track.

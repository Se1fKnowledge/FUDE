# Light on a complex form: drapery

Drapery is intimidating: dozens of folds, each one different. But light on cloth follows the same rules as on a ball. You only need to see simple forms in the cloth.

## A fold is a cylinder

A vertical fold hanging from a rail is almost a cylinder. It has:

- a **ridge** — the convex part, closest to us;
- **slopes** — one faces the light, the other faces away;
- a **depth** — where the cloth goes inward, into the fold.

The light is on the left, so on each fold:

1. the left slope and the ridge are **light**;
2. the right slope is a **halftone** turning into shadow;
3. the depth of the fold is the **darkest**.

## Rhythm

The cloth repeats this pattern fold after fold: light — halftone — shadow — light… This rhythm is what gives the feel of soft, heavy cloth. If all the folds are lit the same, the drapery turns into a striped fence. Vary the width and depth of the folds — the rhythm should breathe.

## Deep and shallow folds

- In a **deep** fold the shadow is dense and the transition sharper.
- A **shallow** fold gives a light halftone without a real shadow.
- Reflected light from the next fold lights up the depth — lesson 4 works here too.

## Order of work

1. An overall mid tone for the whole cloth.
2. Large shadows in the depth of the main folds.
3. Light on the ridges.
4. Halftones that tie light and shadow together.
5. Small folds and reflected light last.

## Sensei's advice

The great masters drew drapery for years: folds of cloth hold everything — form, rhythm, light and softness. Hang a towel over a chair and look at it under a lamp.

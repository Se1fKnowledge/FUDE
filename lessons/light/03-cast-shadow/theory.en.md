# A sphere's cast shadow

The core shadow lies on the object itself. A cast shadow is the shadow the object throws onto the floor, a wall or a neighbouring object. Without it the ball hangs in the air. With it, the ball stands on the table.

## Where the shadow falls

A shadow always falls **away from the light**. Draw an imaginary line from the source through the object — the shadow continues it behind the object.

- The sun at the top left — the shadow goes right.
- The lower the source, the longer the shadow.
- A source straight above the object — the shadow gathers into a patch under it.

## The shape of the shadow

A ball's shadow on the floor is an oval flattened by perspective. It starts **at the point where the ball touches the floor** and stretches away from the light.

## The tone of the shadow

1. **The darkest spot is at the point of contact.** Neither direct light nor light bounced off the floor gets there. This dark crack is often called the contact shadow.
2. **Further away, the shadow gets lighter and softer.** The further from the object, the more light "flows" under the edge of the shadow.
3. **The edge of the cast shadow is crisp at the base and soft in the distance.**

A cast shadow on a lit floor is usually **darker** than the ball's core shadow: the floor and the surroundings light up the ball, while the floor under the ball is closed off from all sides.

## Technique

- A separate «Shadow» layer under the ball layer or above the floor.
- A soft brush, opacity 30–50%: the oval of the shadow, then the dark point of contact.
- The «Multiply» layer mode keeps the floor's colour under the shadow.

## Sensei's advice

Look at a cup on your desk. The thin dark line where the cup touches the desk makes it heavy and real. Find such a line in every drawing.

# Reflected light

Light does not vanish when it hits a surface: part of it bounces off and lights up everything around. That is why shadows are never black — reflected light lights them up.

## What reflected light is

Reflected light is bounced light on the **shadow side** of an object. It appears where the form faces a lit surface: the floor, a wall, a neighbouring object.

On a ball on a light floor, the reflected light is a light arc at the bottom edge of the core shadow. Because of it, the darkest part of the shadow lies not at the edge but at the terminator.

## The main rule

**Reflected light is always darker than any spot on the lit side.** It is part of the shadow. If reflected light becomes as bright as the light, the form breaks: the ball starts to look lit from two sides, or flat.

A typical mistake is a bright "rim" along the whole outline of the shadow. Reflected light:

- lies **inside** the form, not on the outline itself;
- is strongest where the form faces the reflecting surface;
- gradually dissolves into the shadow.

## The colour of reflected light

Bounced light carries the colour of the surface it bounced off:

- a warm wooden floor gives warm reflected light;
- a blue sky lights shadows with a cool tint;
- a red cloth nearby tints the shadow reddish.

This is one of the most beautiful things in painting: an object's surroundings show in its shadows.

## Technique

- A separate «Reflected light» layer.
- A soft brush, opacity 15–25%, a tone slightly lighter than the shadow with a tint of the floor.
- A few arcing strokes along the edge, then a check: is the reflected light darker than the light?

## Sensei's advice

A shadow is where the colours of the surroundings live. Look at the shadow of an object on a windowsill: it is bluish from the sky, not grey.
